## 📝 Exercise 1

Let us indicate what a person’s name is. Suggested subjects are
provided in English. Use a different name (Tamil or English) for
each. Masculine names include **Raaman**, **Goovindan**, **Arasu** and
feminine names: **Lakṣmi**, **Kalyaaṇi**, **Nittilaa**. A correct answer does
not, of course, necessarily mean that you chose the name found in
the key. The Tamil writing system does not distinguish capital
letters and small letters. However, in the Roman transcription used
in this book, to help you distinguish proper nouns (e.g. names of
persons) from common nouns, the former are spelt with a capital
letter.

_Example_: **naan Murugan**. I am Murugan.

1. you
2. he
3. you (polite)
4. professor
5. professor’s student

### Solutions

<Spoiler label="Reveal Solution" hint="Try it yourself first!">

1. nii Goovindan.
2. avan Arasu.
3. niinga Nittilaa.
4. peeraasiriyar Lakṣmi.
5. peeraasiriyarooḍa maaṇavan Raaman.

</Spoiler>

## 📝 Exercise 2

Now provide information on these lines by using the word **peeru**
‘name’ preceded by a possessive form.

_Example_: **naan Murugan**. I am Murugan.

1. niinga
2. en maaṇavan
3. onga maaṇavan

### Solutions

<Spoiler label="Reveal Solution" hint="Try it yourself first!">

1. onga peeru Goovindan.
2. en maaṇavan peeru Arasu.
3. onga maaṇavan peeru Raaman.

</Spoiler>

## 📝 Exercise 3

You are not sure that you have got someone’s name right. Find
out by asking. Remember to use masculine or feminine names in
appropriate places!

_Example_: **niinga Muruganaa?**

1. avan (he)
2. avaru (he (polite))
3. ava (she)
4. onga peeru
5. onga maaṇavan peeru

### Solutions

<Spoiler label="Reveal Solution" hint="Try it yourself first!">

1. avan Goovindanaa?
2. avaru Arasaa?
3. ava Nittilaavaa?
4. onga peeru Lakṣmiyaa?
5. onga maaṇavan peeru Raamanaa?

</Spoiler>
